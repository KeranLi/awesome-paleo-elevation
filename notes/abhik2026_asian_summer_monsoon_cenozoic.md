# A brief history of Asian summer monsoon evolution in the Cenozoic era

**Paper:** Abhik, S., Capitanio, F. A., Goswami, B. N., Farnsworth, A., Clift, P. D., and Dommenget, D. (2026). *npj Climate and Atmospheric Science*. [10.1038/s41612-025-01259-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41612-025-01259-7)

**Status:** Read on 2026-08-30.

## Scope and approach

This review and modelling study examines the Cenozoic evolution of the Asian summer monsoon (ASM), including the South Asian Summer Monsoon (SASM) and East Asian monsoon system. It evaluates the roles of Tibetan Plateau (TP) uplift, India-Asia convergence, Neotethys and Paratethys palaeogeography, atmospheric CO2, and global cooling using geological records and climate-model experiments.

## Main model result

Experiments indicate a threshold-like response in the late Eocene to Oligocene, approximately **38-27 Ma**: the ASM strengthens substantially when the Tibetan Plateau reaches an area-mean elevation above about **3.5 km**. In the model, this elevation increases the upper-tropospheric meridional temperature contrast, promotes a farther-north summer migration of the ITCZ, and reorganizes the circulation that supplies monsoon rainfall.

The simulations suggest that mean TP elevation is a primary control on May-October rainfall once the threshold is crossed. The modeled response is not presented as a direct geological measurement of TP elevation; the 3.5 km value is a sensitivity threshold within the tested experiments.

## Evolutionary timing

The review emphasizes that the timing of ASM intensification remains debated. Proxy syntheses place major strengthening near **27 Ma** or in the early Miocene at approximately **24-22 Ma**, whereas monsoon-like climates in China and Myanmar may extend back into the Paleocene or Eocene. Some reconstructions even trace elements of East Asian monsoon-like circulation to the Cretaceous. These are not necessarily equivalent definitions of the same modern monsoon system.

DeepMIP Eocene simulations indicate that the summer ITCZ migrated only to south of about 15°N in the early Eocene, leaving South Asia relatively dry and the SASM weak. This result is consistent with geochemical evidence discussed by Abhik et al. (2024), Fang et al. (2021), and Xie et al. (2020).

## Mechanism and climate background

The authors argue that both topography and global climate state matter. During the Eocene-Oligocene transition, declining atmospheric CO2 and global cooling altered the background thermal gradient and circulation. In the simulations, early Eocene and early Oligocene monsoon weakness is associated with weaker surface sensible heating and weaker geopotential-height anomalies between 1000 and 500 hPa over the TP.

As the TP rises above the model threshold, the lower-tropospheric low and the meridional temperature contrast strengthen. Both peak in the Aquitanian in the simulations and exceed present-day values, indicating a potentially stronger-than-modern ASM circulation at that time.

## Limitations and interpretation

- The 3.5 km threshold is model-dependent and should not be treated as a direct paleoaltimetry datum.
- Proxy records may document rainfall seasonality, moisture source, or regional circulation rather than one uniquely defined ASM intensity.
- The proposed timing of intensification differs among proxy records and models, partly because “monsoon onset” is defined in different ways.
- Topographic uplift, India-Asia convergence, atmospheric CO2, global cooling, and ocean gateways covary through the Cenozoic, making single-driver attribution difficult.

## Maintainer interpretation

The paper's central contribution is to frame ASM evolution as a threshold response to the combined topographic and climate background state. It supports an important role for TP mean elevation, while showing that the timing and nature of an “Asian monsoon” depend on the diagnostic used and should not be reduced to a single Neogene uplift event.
