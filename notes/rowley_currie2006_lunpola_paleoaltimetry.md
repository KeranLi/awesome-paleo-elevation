# Palaeo-altimetry of the late Eocene to Miocene Lunpola Basin

**Paper:** Rowley, D. B., and Currie, B. S. (2006). *Nature*. [10.1038/nature04506](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04506)

**Status:** Read on 2026-08-27.

## Question

The paper asks how high the central Tibetan Plateau, and specifically the Lunpola Basin region, had risen by the late Eocene to Miocene. It develops a carbonate-water isotope method intended to recover the elevation signal from ancient lake and soil carbonates.

## Study area and archives

The study focuses on the Lunpola Basin in central Tibet. Samples include lacustrine and palaeosol carbonates from the middle and upper Niubao Formation, marls and limestones from the middle Dingqing Formation, and modern small streams sampled along the Amdo-Dongqiao-Lunpola route and within the basin.

The stratigraphic framework used in the paper places the middle Niubao Formation in the Eocene, the upper Niubao Formation from the late Eocene into the Oligocene, and the middle Dingqing Formation in the Miocene.

## Method

Rowley and Currie estimate palaeo-elevation from the relationship between the oxygen-isotope composition of meteoric water and elevation. The approach compares carbonate isotope values with modern water observations and uses the difference between sea-level precipitation and carbonate-derived water to infer an elevation effect.

An important feature is the **hypsometric effect**: water collected by a basin, lake, or groundwater system integrates precipitation across the elevation distribution of its catchment. The resulting isotope value can therefore represent a precipitation-weighted catchment signal rather than the altitude of the sampling point alone.

The authors treat the carbonate samples as micritic and report little petrographic evidence for recrystallization, reducing (but not eliminating) concern about post-depositional alteration.

## Isotope observations

- Middle Dingqing Formation carbonate δ18O values are approximately -8 to -4 per mil.
- Upper Niubao Formation carbonate δ18O is approximately -17.2 ± 1.2 per mil (2σ).
- Middle Niubao Formation carbonate δ18O values are approximately -6 to -2 per mil.

Modern low-latitude and low-elevation precipitation is relatively stable at about -3.6 ± 1.6 per mil in the comparison dataset. The reconstruction uses a lower starting value of about -6 ± 1.6 per mil to accommodate the elevation calculation.

## Main result

The paper concludes that the central Tibetan Plateau had reached elevations **exceeding 4 km by the late Eocene to early Oligocene**, with the authors summarizing central Tibet as being above 4 km since approximately **35 ± 5 Ma**.

The result is interpreted as evidence that substantial plateau elevation was established early, rather than produced only by a late Miocene phase of uplift. The authors also compare the inferred history with India-Asia collision and distributed deformation models, which predict rapid early expansion followed by more stable northward growth.

## Interpretation and limitations

The modern calibration points generally plot below a one-to-one relationship in the authors' calculation. They interpret this as evidence that evaporation and related processes can make the reconstruction underestimate elevation; accordingly, the inferred value may be a **lower bound**.

The hypsometric effect also limits what the number means. A carbonate formed in the Lunpola Basin may integrate water from a broad and elevated catchment, so the estimate should not automatically be read as the local basin-floor elevation. Conversely, source-water changes, evaporation, and imperfect modern analogues can shift carbonate δ18O independently of topography.

Because the age assignments and isotope-elevation calibration are central to the conclusion, later work should check the stratigraphic correlations, the modern-water comparison set, and how uncertainty in source and evaporation propagates into the >4 km estimate.

## Maintainer interpretation

This study is foundational for Lunpola paleoaltimetry because it made the water-source and catchment-integration problem explicit. Its headline result is an early high-elevation central Tibet, but the most transferable lesson is methodological: carbonate isotope values constrain a hydrologic and topographic domain, not necessarily a single point elevation.

## Follow-up checks

- Compare the >4 km by 35 ± 5 Ma estimate with the revised Lunpola chronology in Li et al. (2022).
- Revisit the modern calibration and the magnitude of the evaporation-related bias.
- Track which samples represent palaeosol water, lake water, or integrated catchment runoff.
