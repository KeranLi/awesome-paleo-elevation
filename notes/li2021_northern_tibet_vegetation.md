# Orographic evolution of northern Tibet shaped vegetation and plant diversity in eastern Asia

**Paper:** Li et al. (2021). *Science Advances*. [10.1126/sciadv.abc7741](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abc7741)

**Status:** Read on 2026-08-27.

## Question

The study asks how staged topographic growth across Tibet could have altered Asian climate, East Asian vegetation, and plant diversity from the late Paleogene to early Neogene. It addresses why ecological modernization in East Asia cannot be explained simply by a single, synchronous uplift of the entire Tibetan Plateau.

## Approach

The authors run 18 climate-vegetation sensitivity experiments using alternative palaeogeographic scenarios. The experiments vary the elevation of different Tibetan domains, including the Gangdese Mountains and Lhasa terrane in southern Tibet, the Qiangtang terrane in northern Tibet, and the Songpan-Ganzi terrane in northeastern Tibet. A dynamic global vegetation model (SDGVM) translates the simulated climate fields into biome distributions and plant functional-type diversity.

The model experiments compare hypotheses motivated by proposed topographic histories, rather than directly measuring palaeo-elevation from a geological archive.

## Main result

High southern Tibet alone does not reproduce modern East Asian vegetation. In the scenarios with elevated Gangdese Mountains and Lhasa terrane, much of East Asia remains dominated by deciduous broadleaf forest, with few evergreen broadleaf plants and relatively low functional-type diversity. A generally low plateau produces a similar mismatch.

Raising northern Qiangtang and northeastern Songpan-Ganzi produces a marked transition between about 20 degrees N and 35 degrees N from deciduous to evergreen broadleaf forest. Plant functional-type diversity increases in southeastern Tibet and southeastern East Asia; the Songpan-Ganzi uplift experiments produce the strongest increase in eastern China and yield vegetation patterns closest to the modern distribution.

The simulations also identify an approximate threshold response: when central Tibetan elevation exceeds about 3,000 m, Southeast Asia shifts more abruptly toward evergreen broadleaf vegetation. This is a model sensitivity threshold, not an independent empirical elevation estimate.

## Mechanism

The principal modeled driver is precipitation, especially winter precipitation, rather than temperature. Northern Tibetan uplift increases summer rainfall but produces a larger relative increase in winter rainfall, weakening East Asian monsoon seasonality and reducing dry-season stress.

In the model, higher northern Tibetan topography lowers sea-level pressure over northwestern China and changes the path of cold, dry air associated with the Siberian-Mongolian high. Warmer, moister southerly airflow can then enter northern and southeastern East Asia, converge and rise, increasing moisture supply and winter precipitation. This circulation change favours evergreen broadleaf vegetation and higher plant diversity.

## Interpretation

The authors infer that southern Tibet may already have been high before the Oligocene without immediately creating modern East Asian vegetation and diversity. They instead emphasize continued growth of northern and northeastern Tibet near the Paleogene-Neogene transition as the topographic development most effective at reorganizing winter circulation and East Asian ecosystems.

## Limitations

- The results depend on climate-model boundary conditions and the assumed elevation patterns for each Tibetan domain.
- Vegetation responses reflect modeled temperature and precipitation fields, so the inferred topographic history is indirect.
- The approximately 3,000 m threshold is specific to the tested experiments and should not be treated as a standalone paleo-elevation datum.
- Independent geological, fossil, and proxy records are needed to evaluate the timing and spatial pattern of the proposed topographic growth.

## Maintainer interpretation

This paper is a useful bridge between paleoaltimetry and palaeoecology. It argues that the geographic pattern of uplift matters as much as mean plateau height: northern and northeastern Tibetan topography can alter East Asian winter moisture pathways and thereby affect vegetation and biodiversity.
