# Impacts of Tibetan Plateau and peripheral uplift on the Asian monsoon climate

**Paper:** Ran Zhang, Dabang Jiang, and Zhongshi Zhang (2016). *Quaternary Sciences*. [10.11928/j.issn.1001-7410.2016.04.15](https://doi.org/10.11928/j.issn.1001-7410.2016.04.15)

**Status:** Read on 2026-08-29.

## Question and approach

The paper uses numerical climate simulations to examine how uplift of the Tibetan Plateau core and its surrounding regions affects Asian monsoon precipitation. It places the experiments in the context of three modelling strategies: an idealized whole-plateau uplift, staged uplift, and regionally differentiated uplift.

The key comparison is between a climate without the Tibetan Plateau topographic high and a scenario in which the plateau core is uplifted. The paper is therefore a model-based assessment of climatic effects, not a direct reconstruction of palaeo-elevation.

## Main precipitation result

In the no-plateau experiment, the relatively dry region is concentrated mainly south of about 46°N and extends eastward to the Bohai region, while the Indian Peninsula, Indochina Peninsula, and southeastern China receive comparatively more precipitation.

When the Tibetan Plateau core is uplifted, the dry region expands markedly northward. In the simulation, uplift of the plateau core substantially reduces precipitation north of the plateau.

## Interpretation

The results show that the location and extent of uplift matter for the Asian monsoon climate. Raising the plateau core can reorganize regional moisture transport and create a northward expansion of relatively dry conditions, rather than simply increasing precipitation everywhere around the plateau.

## Limitations

- The result depends on the model configuration and the idealized topographic scenarios.
- The private reading notes do not record the simulated time slice, boundary conditions, or quantitative precipitation changes; these should be checked in the paper before making a more detailed comparison.
- A precipitation response is not itself a direct elevation proxy. It constrains the climatic consequences of an assumed uplift pattern.

## Maintainer interpretation

This study is useful for separating the climatic roles of the Tibetan Plateau core and its margins. Its central message is that core uplift can suppress precipitation to the north and shift the boundary of dry climates northward, highlighting the importance of uplift geometry in monsoon modelling.
