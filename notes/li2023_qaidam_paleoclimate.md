# Eocene to Miocene paleoclimate reconstruction of the northern Tibetan Plateau

**Paper:** Li et al. (2023). *Frontiers in Earth Science*. [10.3389/feart.2023.1217304](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2023.1217304)

**Status:** Read on 2026-08-27.

## Question and study area

The paper reconstructs Eocene to Miocene paleoclimate change in the western Qaidam Basin on the northern Tibetan Plateau. It asks how regional aridification developed and how the record relates to Paratethys Sea retreat, global climate change, and tectonic uplift around northern Tibet.

## Stratigraphic intervals

The discussion focuses on three intervals:

- Upper Xiaganchaigou Formation, approximately 40-32 Ma.
- Basal Xiayoushashan Formation, approximately 22-20 Ma.
- Upper Shangyoushashan Formation, approximately 13-8.2 Ma.

## Evidence and methods

The study combines mineralogical observations with carbon and oxygen isotopes of lacustrine carbonates. It also compares the Qaidam record with carbonate isotope records from Tibet, Qinghai, and Central Asia to place local changes in a broader regional context.

A notable sedimentary signal is the expansion of evaporite minerals in the Qaidam Basin near 35 Ma. Gypsum and halite become abundant, with reported contents increasing from about 3% to 39%. The authors relate this timing to the retreat of the Paratethys Sea.

## Main paleoclimate interpretation

The paper identifies repeated phases of increasing aridity:

- Aridity during approximately 40-32 Ma is discussed in connection with Paratethys Sea regression and Tibetan Plateau uplift.
- Aridity during approximately 22-20 Ma is attributed mainly to uplift and denudation of mountains surrounding the basin.
- Aridity after approximately 13 Ma is interpreted as a combined response to global cooling and tectonic events in northern Tibet.

The study therefore treats aridification as a coupled climate-tectonic process rather than as a simple monotonic response to one uplift event.

## Relation to paleo-elevation

The paper provides regional context for paleo-elevation research, but the principal result is a paleoclimate and aridity reconstruction rather than a single quantitative elevation estimate. Tectonic uplift is used as one possible driver of changing moisture balance, while mineral precipitation and isotope values also respond to basin hydrology, evaporation, and the changing source and circulation of moisture.

## Limitations and follow-up

- Evaporite abundance is a strong indicator of basin aridification, but it is not an elevation proxy by itself.
- Carbonate δ13C and δ18O can reflect evaporation, water balance, source changes, and lake hydrology in addition to topography.
- The relative timing of Paratethys retreat and northern Tibetan uplift should be checked against independent stratigraphic and tectonic chronologies.
- The paper recommends comparison with Bosboom et al. (2014a, 2014b) for the history of Paratethys retreat and its regional climatic effects.

## Maintainer interpretation

This paper is useful as a process-oriented companion to isotope paleoaltimetry. It shows why aridification and evaporite development in the Qaidam Basin should not be converted directly into an elevation history without separating tectonic forcing from basin-scale evaporation, moisture-source change, and global cooling.
